# Source Map

This skill is source-informed, not a copy of any one source. Use the links below when current citations or deeper reference hunting are needed.

## Film Grammar

- StudioBinder, camera movement guide: `https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/different-types-of-camera-movements-in-film/`
  - Useful for push-in, pull-out, zoom, dolly zoom, roll, tracking, arc, handheld, and their story functions.
- StudioBinder, camera angles guide: `https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/types-of-camera-shot-angles-in-film/`
  - Useful for low angle, high angle, dutch angle, overhead, eye-level, and power/vulnerability framing.
- StudioBinder, shot size guide: `https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/types-of-camera-shots-sizes-in-film/`
  - Useful for matching close-up / wide / medium / extreme close-up to emotion and information.
- StudioBinder, 50+ camera shots/angles/techniques: `https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/ultimate-guide-to-camera-shots/`
  - Useful for shot size, camera angle, framing, movement, mechanisms, depth of field, and shot lists.
- StudioBinder, composition guide: `https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/rules-of-shot-composition-in-film/`
  - Useful for rule of thirds, symmetry, leading lines, blocking, depth of field, deep space.
- Boords, camera shots and angles guide: `https://boords.com/blog/16-types-of-camera-shots-and-angles-with-gifs`
  - Useful for storyboard-friendly shot vocabulary.
- Boords, storyboard guide: `https://boords.com/how-to-storyboard`
  - Useful for turning a script/idea into shots and a shot list.
- Boords, shot list template: `https://boords.com/shot-list-template`
  - Useful for shot-list fields: scene, setup, shot, camera angle, shot size, movement, lens, notes.
- StoryboardArt, camera angles for storyboard artists: `https://storyboardart.org/storyboard-tutorials/camera-angles-for-storyboard-artists/`
  - Useful for starting storyboard choices from wide / medium / close-up and then refining.
- Filmmakers Academy, negative space guide: `https://www.filmmakersacademy.com/blog-negative-space-film/`
  - Useful for isolation, vulnerability, emotional pressure, and scale.
- StudioBinder, rhythm in editing: `https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/how-does-an-editor-control-the-rhythm-of-a-film/`
  - Useful for shot duration, intensity, relief, and emotional pacing.
- StudioBinder, Walter Murch Rule of Six: `https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/walter-murch-rule-of-six/`
  - Useful for emotion-first cuts, story advancement, rhythm, eye trace, and spatial continuity.
- StudioBinder, editing transitions: `https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/types-of-editing-transitions-in-film/`
  - Useful for match cut, jump cut, invisible cut, and transition mechanics.
- Adobe, cuts in film: `https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/cuts-in-film.html`
  - Useful for practical cut types such as match cut, jump cut, smash cut and pacing effects.
- Adobe, speed ramp guide: `https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/hub/guides/premiere-pro-speed-ramp.html`
  - Useful for the concept of keyframed speed changes and speed ramping for action energy.

## AI Video Prompting

- Runway, Gen-4 Video Prompting Guide: `https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/39789879462419-Gen-4-Video-Prompting-Guide`
  - Useful for camera motion, style descriptors, and image-to-video motion language.
- Runway, Gen-3 Alpha Prompting Guide: `https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/30586818553107-Gen-3-Alpha-Prompting-Guide`
  - Useful for concise/direct motion prompting and avoiding negative phrasing in the main prompt.
- Luma, Video Models Field Guide: `https://lumalabs.ai/learning-center/articles/luma-video-models-field-guide`
  - Useful for temporal movement, specific camera movement, and avoiding vague beauty descriptors.
