# Progressive Transformation Frontier

Use this for biomechanical armor, cyborg assembly, skin-to-machine replacement, creature armor growth, magical material conversion with visible causality, or any transformation that must not read as a flash-cut skin swap.

## Readable State Model

Keep three states visible whenever possible:

```text
unchanged old surface
| active transformation frontier |
completed new material
```

The frontier must have a location, direction, speed, and visible source.

Examples:

- eyes ignite, facial seams pre-crack, and the frontier travels from jaw to cheek to temple;
- energy follows the neck and clavicle before shoulder armor closes;
- flesh expands and splits, inner fibers appear, then outer shell plates turn and harden;
- skin granulates away, an internal mechanical spine appears, then ceramic plates overlap and lock over it.

## Causal Stage Order

```text
behavioral trigger
-> local activation mark
-> pre-crack, swelling, seam light, or tension
-> old surface exits by splitting, peeling, retracting, dissolving, or shedding
-> internal structure becomes visible
-> new material grows, slides, folds, deposits, or clamps along the frontier
-> panels overlap and lock
-> silhouette-defining structures deploy last
-> energy decays and material settles
```

Do not skip directly from trigger to finished form.

## Material Behavior Assignment

Give each material one transformation verb family:

- skin: crack, peel, granulate, retract, shed;
- flesh/fiber: swell, stretch, split, tense, scar over;
- internal metal/bone: reveal, extend, align, articulate;
- ceramic armor: deposit, grow from an edge, slide, overlap, clamp;
- shell/chitin: extrude, unfold, harden, interlock;
- cloth or soft covering: tear, retract, wrap, become trapped beneath plates;
- energy: trace seams, jump between anchors, illuminate the frontier, decay after locking.

Avoid making every layer melt, glow, and grow in the same way.

## Camera Rule

- Use a relatively stable close or medium-close view.
- Let the body turn or the camera make a small arc to reveal the next region.
- Follow the active frontier through face, neck, chest, shoulder, arm, or weapon.
- Widen only when the new silhouette, balance, or complete form must be proven.
- Do not combine transformation, rapid orbit, repeated cuts, and full-body effect flooding in the same fragile generation.

## Occlusion Rule

A sourced flash, smoke burst, spark shower, passing limb, cloth wipe, or body turn may hide the hardest topology change for an instant.

After the occlusion:

- identity and subject count remain stable;
- the new structure connects continuously to the last visible boundary;
- background and camera side remain recognizable;
- the transformation frontier continues rather than restarting elsewhere.

## Completion Proof

Finish with:

- final faceplate, horn, wing, shoulder, tail, blade, or outer silhouette component;
- visible clamp, seam closure, tension lock, or biological hardening;
- one residual shudder, breath, heat plume, fragment fall, electrical decay, or plate micro-adjustment;
- a short stable pose that proves the final form without freezing for an excessive duration.

## Negative Constraints

```text
No full-body simultaneous morph, no flash-cut replacement, no unrelated finished model,
no sustained effect cloud hiding the subject, no duplicated armor, no random material
swapping, no frontier teleporting between body regions, no unchanged background light
despite strong local emission, no instant action before the new structure locks and settles.
```
