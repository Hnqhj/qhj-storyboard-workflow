# TF Mecha Storyboard Import

Use this reference when a transformation is also a fight scene, storyboard, or AI-video prompt: vehicle-to-robot, robot-to-weapon, armor deployment, mechanical body reconfiguration, mecha duel, or a "Transformers-like" sequence that must read as physical restructuring instead of magic skin swapping.

## 1. Core stance

Do not write "the car becomes a robot" or "the armor transforms" as a single event.

Treat transformation as physical reconstruction:

```text
unlock -> panel separation -> internal frame exposure -> axis rotation -> telescoping/extension -> load transfer -> regrip/rebrace -> lock -> damping -> combat-ready proof
```

For mecha combat, the scene is not only "big robots fighting"; it is tactical opposition where mechanical state, weapon line, body mass, and terrain all change the fight.

## 2. Nine-grid phase logic

For storyboard or image-board outputs, use a 9-grid structure when the user asks for a full transformation beat or iconic mecha action.

```text
1. Establish original form, scale, environment, and threat.
2. Trigger: braking, impact, command, overload, jump, weapon contact, or incoming attack.
3. First rupture: seams open, panels separate, old silhouette starts to break.
4. Internal mechanism reveal: spine, actuator, rails, turbine, core, weapon mount.
5. Major mass migration: torso/limbs/weapon modules rotate, slide, telescope, or unfold.
6. Load-bearing proof: feet, hands, landing gear, wheels, stabilizers, or frame braces take weight.
7. Combat contact: block, parry, punch, grapple, slide, landing, or weapon line engages.
8. Consequence: recoil, armor stress, sparks, terrain damage, opponent displacement.
9. Final locked form: readable silhouette, residual vibration, new handling behavior, next-action hook.
```

Do not leave frames 6-9 as generic "cool final shots". They must contain mechanical detail, contact points, force feedback, or changed combat information.

## 3. Mechanical detail checklist

Every transformation beat should specify:

- invariant identity anchors: color block, core, cockpit, faceplate, emblem, weapon, silhouette spine;
- where the added length/volume comes from;
- which parts rotate, slide, fold, telescope, separate, latch, or lock;
- exposed internal structure: piston, rail, hinge, gear, cable, turbine, energy conduit;
- mass transfer: what becomes load-bearing and when;
- center-of-mass change and stance compensation;
- friction, inertia, heat, spark, vibration, metal flex, hydraulic pressure;
- final stable proof: foot plant, hand brace, joint lock, recoil damping, servo whine, dust settling.

## 4. Mecha combat rules

When transformation happens during a fight:

1. The transformation must solve a tactical problem:
   - evade impact;
   - shorten/extend reach;
   - change weapon line;
   - increase armor coverage;
   - shift from speed form to load-bearing form;
   - create a block, trap, grapple, or launch.
2. Keep one readable force path per shot.
3. Show support points. Giant forms need ground proof, recoil proof, and braking proof.
4. The opponent must react to the mechanical change. Otherwise the transformation is decorative.
5. Terrain must record mass: cracked pavement, compressed soil, sparks on metal, dust plume, water displacement.

```text

```

Paste-ready wording:

```text
The transformation begins from the existing chassis rather than appearing from nowhere: seams split along the side panels, inner rails extend, shoulder assemblies rotate out on visible hinges, piston clusters push the torso upward, wheel hubs fold behind the calves, the center of mass drops as both feet lock into the ground, residual vibration and dust prove the new weight before the mecha enters the next attack.
```

Negative constraints:

```text
no instant morph, no magic growth, no unrelated extra parts, no duplicated limbs, no weightless floating, no unchanged handling after transformation, no full-body simultaneous blur hiding the mechanism
```
