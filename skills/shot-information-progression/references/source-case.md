# Source Conversation Case

## Evidence inspected

A long chat screenshot supplied by Liu on 2026-07-18. The conversation discussed film atmosphere, lighting examples, storyboard logic, software-generated shot sequences, and development teams copying finished films.

## Retained observations

1. A film image must foreground explicit information through a person, object, scene, or emotionally legible atmosphere.
2. A beautiful light pattern without subject, trace, state change, or emotional carrier is harder to read and easier to mistake for mere lighting display.
3. Film ultimately organizes emotion, but emotion should be expressed through visible carriers rather than labels.
4. Drawn action is observable behavior; the hidden purpose and attitude behind it create emotional meaning. A shot therefore has a visible and a meaning layer.
5. Even before performance, lighting, and color, one shot already contains many decisions: scene, subject, camera position, angle, content, movement, and destination. These choices should serve an information point.
6. A storyboard is the foundation of visual narration, not a deterministic forecast of the final film. Shooting and editing alter the original design.
7. Copying a finished film backward does not reliably recover its initial storyboard or causal design process.
8. A camera choice may carry several possible meanings; the director's authorship lies in choosing and ordering those meanings for the current context.
9. Tool abundance without purpose produces a stitched-together result: many means, no reason, no coherent outcome.
10. Lighting, color, atmosphere, and style are later amplification layers. They cannot substitute for image narration.
11. In the cited software demo, a push into an already-angry face followed by another view of the same anger repeated information. The next shot should instead reveal what the father sees, the receiver, the cause, or the consequence.

## Portable mechanism

The reusable unit is an Information Delta Gate: every shot needs one primary information carrier, an observable behavior/state, an implied meaning, and a handoff. Every following shot must add, answer, reframe, contradict, escalate, withhold, or show consequence.

## Boundaries

The conversation expresses craft judgments rather than a full production standard. It does not prove that every people-free shot is weak, that every shot must contain only one literal object, or that storyboards should be discarded. Empty space, repetition, abstraction, and atmosphere remain valid when their information or emotional function is explicit.
