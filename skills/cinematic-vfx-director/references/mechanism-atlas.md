# Cinematic VFX Mechanism Atlas

Use this atlas after the shot function and physical action are already clear. Select a branch; do not paste the whole file into a prompt.

## 1. Impact and Force Propagation

Core chain:

```text
preload/pressure -> exact contact -> force direction -> receiver displacement ->
environmental wave -> short residue
```

Useful mechanisms:

- local air compression or refraction before contact;
- one directional contact flash, cone, wedge, or crescent rather than a generic circular burst;
- one-frame overshoot, impact image, or selective hitstop for a hero hit;
- delayed debris and cloth/hair response that follow the force direction;
- camera impulse after contact, then decay;
- expression, breath, guard interruption, and synchronized material sound.

Avoid making sparks or shake the only evidence of force.

## 2. Motion, Trails, and Speed

Choose the mechanism by proof task:

| Need to prove | Best first choice |
|---|---|
| Real camera speed | shutter-aware blur + parallax + physical wake |
| Exaggerated animation speed | designed smear + pose echo |
| Weapon arc | anchored ribbon/trail with clear start and cutoff |
| Sudden direction change | wake bend + braking debris + body counter-rotation |
| Extreme acceleration | reference-object parallax + compression at start + stretched wake |
| Graphic 2D intensity | speed/focus lines + contour break + impact image |

Trail rules:

- start at the actual moving edge or emitter;
- inherit source velocity and path;
- preserve front/back occlusion;
- thin, break, cool, or disperse after the source passes;
- never outlive the event without a physical residue reason.

## 3. Energy and Magic

Build identity from more than color:

- source object/body region/ritual;
- shape family: calligraphic stroke, feather geometry, crystalline facets, fluid ribbon, geometric seal, smoke claw, etc.;
- motion grammar: pulse, coil, lash, orbit, collapse, grow, fracture, inhale;
- material analogy: plasma, ink, glass, ash, silk, metal engraving, compressed air;
- environmental effect and decay signature.

Core chain:

```text
source activation -> coherent shape formation -> directed travel/field behavior ->
collision or state change -> environmental light/pressure response -> decay/residue
```

Threat identity should remain readable in silhouette and timing even if color is desaturated.

## 4. Destruction and Debris

Start from material and structural constraints:

- stone: massy chunks, dust, spall, crack propagation;
- metal: bending, tearing, fasteners/constraints failing, sparks only at friction or electrical sources;
- glass: sharp fracture network, sheets and shards, bright edge catches;
- wood: grain-directed splitting, fibers, splinters, dust;
- wet earth: clods, mud displacement, water and darkened residue;
- cloth/paper: tearing, flutter, drag, low mass and long settling.

Debris should emit from newly separated or impacted surfaces, inherit local velocity, collide, and settle. Build bulk motion and silhouette before micro-noise.

## 5. Smoke, Fire, Water, Weather, and Atmosphere

Direct the large-scale shape first:

- source and emission direction;
- density/temperature/fuel or water volume/pressure;
- wind/flow field and obstacle interaction;
- silhouette and timing beats;
- cooling, dissipation, evaporation, settling, or pooling.

Use noise only after the bulk motion reads. Large masses usually need slower secondary motion than small sprays to feel large.

## 6. Transformation, Growth, and Corruption

Avoid dissolve-only transformation. Define:

```text
trigger -> active frontier -> material conversion/growth -> load redistribution ->
new silhouette -> lock/settle -> residual evidence
```

The frontier may be a crack, seam, growth edge, folding mechanism, spreading mark, or material phase change. Existing parts must be pushed, folded, displaced, consumed, or reconfigured rather than simply replaced.

## 7. Compositing and Scene Integration

An integrated effect needs:

- correct depth and holdout/occlusion;
- nearby light spill and shadow response;
- reflection/refraction only where the material supports it;
- shared exposure, black level, atmosphere, grain/noise, and color transform;
- motion blur/smear compatible with the source layer;
- foreground/midground/background separation.

Light wrap, glow, grain, and haze are integration tools, not proof of quality. Use them selectively.
