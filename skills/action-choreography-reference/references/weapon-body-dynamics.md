# Weapon And Body Dynamics

Use this reference when an action must communicate weapon weight, center of mass, balance, inertia, leverage, traction, recoil, braking, or physically distinct character strength.

This is a cinematic biomechanics model for fictional choreography and AI prompting. It is not a real-world combat instruction manual.

## 1. Physical Profile

Define these before naming a martial art:

```text
actor mass class: light / medium / heavy / giant
actor strength relative to weapon: overpowered / matched / strained / mechanically assisted
weapon total-mass impression: light / medium / heavy / extreme
weapon mass distribution: grip-biased / balanced / tip-heavy / head-heavy / flexible-delayed
weapon length and leverage: compact / body-length / long-pole / oversized
grip: one hand / two hands / sliding grip / braced / harnessed
base of support: narrow / shoulder-width / wide / split stance / kneeling / grounded anchor
dominant action: redirect / accelerate / carry-through / brace / absorb / recover
```

Do not use exact kilograms unless the story needs them. Relative load is more useful:

```text
weapon load ratio = weapon demand relative to the actor's strength and control
```

The same weapon should move differently in the hands of a fragile human, trained fighter, powered armor, giant robot, or superhuman synthetic.

## 2. Force And Balance Chain

Build force visibly from support to the prop:

```text
ground or support contact
-> ankle/knee adjustment
-> hip and pelvis transfer
-> trunk rotation or bracing
-> shoulder and arm guidance
-> grip transmits force
-> weapon accelerates
-> contact or near miss
-> recoil returns through the same chain
-> feet, stance, or route absorb the remainder
```

Visual rules:

- Keep the projected body center of mass supported by the feet, hand brace, wall, vehicle, cable, or other named contact.
- A narrow stance permits fast redirection but less resistance to a large off-axis load.
- A wider or split stance communicates braking, bracing, and resistance to torque.
- Lowering the pelvis increases the visual impression of stability only when knees, ankles, and foot contact also respond.
- If the center of mass leaves the support area, require a step, fall, slide, hand plant, cable tension, or deliberate airborne phase.
- Foot traction must have evidence: sole compression, floor grip, dust, water displacement, skid, surface deformation, or controlled pivot.

## 3. Weapon Mass Distribution

Total mass alone does not define handling. Mass farther from the grip increases rotational inertia and makes starts, reversals, and stops slower.

### Grip-Biased

Visual behavior:

- quick initiation;
- short redirection;
- small recovery;
- fine wrist and forearm corrections remain visible.

Useful for:

- knives;
- short swords;
- compact mechanical weapons;
- hand guards.

### Balanced Near The Hands

Visual behavior:

- moderate start and brake;
- clean changes between attack and guard;
- body rotation contributes without dominating.

Useful for:

- arming swords;
- medium staffs;
- paired weapons;
- versatile fantasy weapons.

### Tip-Heavy Or Head-Heavy

Visual behavior:

- visible preload before launch;
- shoulders and hips commit early;
- weapon head lags during startup, then carries through;
- missed actions pull the body farther;
- recovery needs a step, circular continuation, grounded catch, or delayed reset.

Useful for:

- axes;
- hammers;
- cleavers;
- giant blades;
- weighted polearms.

### Long-Pole Distribution

Visual behavior:

- hands separate to create leverage;
- the rear hand drives while the lead hand guides;
- the far tip has delayed response and a large spatial arc;
- turns require more room and an earlier brake;
- grip may slide to change reach and rotational demand.

Useful for:

- spears;
- staffs;
- glaives;
- giant bows;
- long mechanical tools.

### Flexible-Delayed

Visual behavior:

- the handle starts first;
- the weighted end follows later;
- tension travels along rope, chain, cloth, cable, or articulated segments;
- release and recovery include a delayed second wave;
- the user must manage orbit, slack, tension, and return.

Useful for:

- chains;
- rope darts;
- meteor hammers;
- whips;
- cable weapons.

## 4. Action Phase Contract

Every weighted action should contain:

```text
support/preload
-> initiation
-> acceleration
-> peak relation
-> follow-through
-> braking
-> recovery into a new stable state
```

### Support / Preload

Show:

- feet widening or aligning;
- pelvis lowering or coiling;
- grip changing;
- weapon settling under gravity;
- cables becoming taut;
- breath or machinery loading.

### Initiation

Show which segment moves first:

- foot drive;
- hip turn;
- shoulder drop;
- hand pull;
- weapon head lag;
- mechanical assist engaging.

### Acceleration

Show coordinated segment sequencing rather than all joints moving at once.

### Peak Relation

The peak can be:

- contact;
- near miss;
- block or bind;
- maximum extension;
- release;
- change of direction;
- environmental impact.

### Follow-Through

The path continues after the peak. The amount depends on inertia and whether the action hit, missed, was redirected, or mechanically stopped.

### Braking

Braking is not a frozen pose. Show:

- opposite foot pressure;
- knee and hip absorption;
- grip tightening or sliding;
- torso counter-rotation;
- weapon arc rising or circling into guard;
- mechanical dampers, locks, or ground anchors;
- small secondary vibration settling.

### Recovery

End in a changed relation:

- new distance;
- new facing;
- new guard;
- shifted initiative;
- altered elevation;
- weapon reset;
- route opened or blocked.

## 5. Contact And Reaction

Treat contact as a shared system:

```text
incoming body/weapon momentum
-> contact point
-> weapon deflection or compression
-> receiver displacement
-> attacker recoil
-> environment response
-> both systems settle
```

Require at least two visible consequences:

- receiver moves, bends, rotates, slides, drops, or changes guard;
- attacker hand, arm, torso, stance, or weapon rebounds;
- weapon flexes, vibrates, binds, glances, or changes arc;
- floor, wall, water, dust, cloth, hair, debris, or vehicle suspension reacts;
- sound has transient, body resonance, and decay.

Do not use camera shake or sparks as a substitute for physical response.

For blocks and binds:

- lock the contact location and weapon orientation;
- show both bodies adjusting to the torque;
- keep the bind brief unless it is the dramatic objective;
- resolve into separation, redirection, loss of grip, or changed line.

For misses:

- the weapon still carries inertia;
- the body must continue, step, pivot, lower, or deliberately redirect;
- do not let a heavy weapon stop instantly in empty air.

## 6. Weapon Families

| Family | Start | Peak | Brake/Recovery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| knife/short blade | compact, little preload | short linear or tight arc | quick return near body |
| medium sword | foot/hip initiate, hands guide | clear edge line | moderate follow-through into guard |
| greatsword | two-hand support, broad preload | body and blade commit together | delayed recovery, step or circular reset |
| axe/hammer | weapon head visibly loads behind | head carries through target line | long brake, body follows, ground or wide stance absorbs |
| spear | rear foot and rear hand drive | tip leads on one line | retract or redirect with whole stance |
| staff | alternating hands and body rotation | continuous arc or thrust | flow into opposite side rather than dead stop |
| shield | asymmetric load changes torso and stance | brace, redirect, shove, cover | shoulder/hip absorb, feet re-square |
| bow | stable bow arm, opening shoulder line | release and string recoil | small hand/limb vibration, posture remains controlled |
| giant bow | named brace/anchor/mechanical assist | stored tension releases | frame shudder, cable recoil, actor resists pull |
| chain/flexible | hand orbit begins first | weighted end arrives late | tension wave returns, orbit controlled before next action |
| dual weapons | separate left/right jobs | one acts while one guards or follows | avoid symmetric windmilling; recover to distinct roles |
| transforming weapon | current form has explicit mass profile | transformation changes silhouette and inertia | actor regrips/rebalances before using new form |

## 7. Character Strength Classes

### Light / Agile

```text
uses angle, timing, foot displacement, sliding grip, deflection, and continuous redirection; avoids dead stops against larger mass
```

### Human-Matched

```text
weapon and body demand visible whole-body coordination; one clean peak followed by readable recovery
```

### Heavy / Armored

```text
slower initiation, strong traction, small unnecessary motion, high stability, substantial environmental reaction, deliberate braking
```

### Superhuman / Synthetic

Do not erase physics. Change the scale of response:

```text
the actor can accelerate a heavy weapon faster, but the floor, joints, clothing, air, target, and weapon structure must receive the increased forces; precision comes from active damping, not weightlessness
```

### Giant / Creature

```text
force reaches terrain and atmosphere before secondary bodies; startup and settling occupy visible time; small opponents respond to pressure waves, route loss, or environmental change
```

## 8. Camera And Sound Readability

Camera:

- Use full-body or wide framing to show feet, support, long arcs, large weapons, throws, and braking.
- Hold through the contact and receiver reaction.
- Use close-ups for one grip change, foot brace, cable tension, joint compression, or vibration detail.
- Keep the camera calmer for heavier motion; body and environment should sell mass.
- Use only brief micro-shake at contact when appropriate.

Sound:

- startup: cloth strain, boot pressure, motor spool, chain tension, breath;
- peak: material-specific transient;
- body: low-mid resonance appropriate to size;
- environment: delayed floor, wall, debris, water, or air response;
- recovery: scrape, cable return, armor settling, weapon ring, breath release;
- silence before a heavy peak can increase perceived mass.

## 9. Prompt Packaging

Use this block before timed action:

```text
Action physics:
Actor mass/strength: [class and relation to weapon].
Weapon dynamics: [length, grip, mass distribution, relative load].
Support: [feet/hand/ground/wall/vehicle/cable contact].
Force chain: [support -> pelvis/trunk -> arms -> weapon].
Peak: [contact/near miss/release and visible response].
Inertia resolution: [follow-through, brake, recovery, final stance].
Environment response: [one or two sourced consequences].
```

Compact prompt example:

```text
The weapon is head-heavy relative to the fighter. A wide split stance and two-handed grip preload the swing; the rear foot and hips initiate while the weapon head visibly lags, then accelerates through one broad arc. On contact both bodies recoil, the lead foot slides several centimeters, dust lifts after the impact, and the weapon continues into a delayed circular brake before settling into a low guard. Keep the full body and weapon arc readable; no instant stop, weightless flutter, sliding feet, or camera shake replacing impact.
```

## 10. Failure Diagnosis

| Symptom | Missing control | Next retry variable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| weapon feels weightless | mass distribution absent | state grip-relative balance and delayed start/stop |
| feet slide without cause | support/traction absent | lock stance and surface reaction |
| heavy weapon moves like a knife | inertia class wrong | lengthen preload, follow-through, and brake |
| actor freezes after impact | no inertia resolution | add body recoil and recovery path |
| contact feels fake | receiver response absent | add equal-and-opposite reaction and changed spacing |
| superhuman looks floaty | strength erased physics | move force into ground, air, prop, and environment |
| all martial arts look identical | style name without mechanics | select posture, force path, distance, rhythm, and recovery signature |
| transformed weapon feels unchanged | new mass profile absent | require regrip, rebalance, and new timing |
| camera makes force unreadable | feet/contact hidden | widen framing and hold through reaction |
| sound feels generic | no material/size envelope | define source, transient, resonance, and decay |

## 11. Research Basis

Mechanisms translated into cinematic controls:

- Balance depends on the changing relation between whole-body center of mass and base of support; gait initiation deliberately perturbs that relation before stepping.  
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5696609/
- Boxing studies connect punch performance with lower-limb force development and sequential acceleration/braking through the body.  
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2022.1099682/full
- Bat studies show that rotational handling depends strongly on moment of inertia around the swing axis, not only total mass.  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29651903/
- Comparative roundhouse-kick research found shared movement components while disciplines differed in center-of-mass and segment-velocity patterns.  
  https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182645
- Maximal hand-speed research found distinct contributions from trunk, shoulder, and elbow rather than one simultaneous rigid motion.  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19759324/
