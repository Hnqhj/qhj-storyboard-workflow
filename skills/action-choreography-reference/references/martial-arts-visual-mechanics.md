# Martial Arts Visual Mechanics

Use this reference to proactively choose a martial-art, dance, sport, stunt, weapon, or partner-movement name anchor and verify it through visible mechanics. Preserve the selected name in the prompt.

This map supports fictional choreography and AI video prompting. It describes posture, movement quality, distance, rhythm, and cinematic silhouette; it does not teach practical harm.

## 1. Selection Fields

For every selected style, define:

```text
range: contact / close / medium / long
stance: square / bladed / upright / low / mobile / rooted
force path: linear / rotational / dropping / rising / whipping / continuous
rhythm: single decisive / combinations / interception / pressure / flow / burst-reset
defense: evade / redirect / cover / parry / absorb / grapple
footwork: advance-retreat / angle step / pivot / circle / level change / crossing step
recovery signature: snap back / continue through / settle rooted / flow into next arc
```

## Action Grammar Priority

Choose the movement grammar before writing event verbs.

```text
event layer: run -> climb -> attack -> evade -> finish
grammar layer: how the body repeatedly organizes those events
```

The grammar layer should control:

- recurring posture and body level;
- dominant line or geometry: linear, circular, diagonal, axial, spiraling;
- support changes: two feet, one foot, hand support, wall/creature contact, airborne;
- rhythm: burst-reset, continuous flow, broken rhythm, heavy punctuation;
- recovery: return to guard, continue through an arc, root, roll, redirect, relaunch;
- silhouette motif: low crescent, inverted V, long diagonal, square armored frame.

Example:

```text
generic: run toward the giant, climb it, strike repeatedly

designed:
Capoeira-inspired low-to-high circular movement with parkour route contact:
the fighter enters through a low lateral sway, plants a hand to redirect around
the sweeping limb, converts the same circular momentum into a wall/creature
step, changes level through an inverted silhouette, and lets each blade action
continue into the next foothold rather than resetting upright.
```

A named style supplies a strong movement prior. Keep it, and make sure its visible mechanics persist across several events.

## 2. Striking Systems

### Boxing

Visual signature:

- compact guard;
- rear-foot and hip contribution;
- direct hand paths mixed with hooks;
- head movement and angle changes;
- quick return to guard.

Best for:

- grounded modern impact;
- readable two-person exchanges;
- tight camera coverage.

Avoid:

- punches thrown only from the arm;
- huge theatrical windups;
- defender remaining static.

### Muay Thai

Visual signature:

- upright pressure;
- shin, knee, elbow, and clinch silhouettes;
- strong hip turn;
- visible posture control at close range;
- heavier single beats than boxing flurries.

Best for:

- brutal close-range pressure;
- compact corridors;
- body and costume contact.

Avoid:

- every strike becoming a flying attack;
- tangled clinch limbs with no posture relation.

### Kickboxing / Sanda

Visual signature:

- hand combinations linked to kicks;
- active entry and exit;
- catches, sweeps, or throws may change level and distance;
- practical cross-range rhythm.

Best for:

- mixed striking exchanges;
- mobile arena or street choreography.

### Karate

Visual signature:

- clear chamber and line;
- sharp acceleration into a decisive beat;
- strong stance punctuation;
- fast reset or controlled finish.

Best for:

- graphic single attacks;
- precision and disciplined spacing.

Avoid:

- holding formal poses too long unless ritual is the point.

### Taekwondo

Visual signature:

- rapid leg chamber;
- high and spinning kick silhouettes;
- fast pelvic rotation;
- frequent height and direction changes;
- light recovery steps.

Best for:

- elegant high-line action;
- fashion, anime, and athletic showcases.

Avoid:

- endless spinning;
- hidden landing or support foot.

### Wing Chun

Visual signature:

- compact centerline structure;
- elbows close;
- short advancing steps;
- interception, hand fighting, and sustained forward pressure;
- little wasted backswing.

Best for:

- narrow spaces;
- intimate close-range exchanges;
- fast tactical-looking hand rhythms.

Avoid:

- windmill arms;
- hands moving while torso and feet remain dead.

### Jeet Kune Do

Visual signature:

- interception;
- broken rhythm;
- direct stop-hit feeling;
- adaptable stance and quick entry-exit;
- economy over ornament.

Best for:

- intelligent reactive protagonists;
- sudden initiative changes.

### Bajiquan-Inspired

Visual signature:

- compact explosive entry;
- low rooted base;
- shoulder, elbow, hip, and whole-frame collision silhouettes;
- short-distance force with a strong final body line.

Best for:

- armored or powerful characters;
- doorway, corridor, or body-check beats.

Avoid:

- turning it into long floating combinations.

### Xingyiquan-Inspired

Visual signature:

- direct forward intention;
- whole-body alignment behind one line;
- compressed preparation and decisive advance;
- little decorative circling.

Best for:

- relentless characters;
- spear-like body action;
- strong screen-direction choreography.

### Baguazhang-Inspired

Visual signature:

- circular stepping;
- changing facing around the opponent;
- palms and torso guide redirection;
- continuous angle acquisition rather than frontal collision.

Best for:

- evasive mystical or strategic fighters;
- orbiting staging with stable axis control.

Avoid:

- camera orbit replacing the performer's actual circling.

### Taijiquan-Inspired

Visual signature:

- calm connected structure;
- absorb, redirect, and issue as one continuous relation;
- apparent softness followed by a clear displacement;
- rooted lower body and controlled upper-body arcs.

Best for:

- power reversal;
- elder/master archetypes;
- contrast between quiet setup and sudden consequence.

Avoid:

- generic slow motion with no opponent pressure or state change.

### Capoeira

Visual signature:

- low-to-high circular pathways;
- hand-supported inversions;
- continuous deceptive rhythm;
- evasive body level changes.

Best for:

- stylish movement;
- circular threats;
- acrobatic evasion.

## 3. Grappling And Ground Systems

### Wrestling

Visual signature:

- lowered level;
- forward drive;
- hand fighting;
- body locks;
- clear pressure into floor, wall, or boundary.

Best for:

- mass contests;
- captures;
- creature or armored-body control.

### Judo

Visual signature:

- grip and posture relation;
- off-balance before the throw;
- close entry;
- one clear rotation or reap;
- receiver follows a readable fall arc.

Best for:

- elegant throws;
- costume-driven grip choreography;
- power reversals.

### Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu-Inspired Ground Scramble

Visual signature:

- hooks, frames, hip movement, positional transitions, and controlled body layering;
- lower tempo than striking but constant leverage changes.

Best for:

- capture, restraint, escape, or survival scenes.

Keep it cinematic:

- show broad positional change rather than detailed submission instruction.

### Aikido / Jujutsu-Inspired Redirection

Visual signature:

- entry off the attack line;
- wrist/arm relation as a visible guide;
- body turn redirects momentum;
- attacker travels past the defender.

Best for:

- non-brutal control;
- calm characters;
- using an opponent's commitment as movement.

Avoid:

- attacker flying without a committed entry or visible leverage.

## 4. Weapon Systems

### Fencing

Visual signature:

- narrow linear lane;
- blade point controls distance;
- rapid advance-retreat;
- compact parry-riposte rhythm.

Best for:

- elegant duels;
- long-lens profile staging;
- precise initiative changes.

### HEMA Longsword

Visual signature:

- two-hand leverage;
- guards define threat lines;
- cuts, thrusts, binds, and winding change blade relation;
- footwork and blade contact resolve together.

Best for:

- grounded armored duels;
- visible contact and torque.

### Kendo / Kenjutsu-Inspired

Visual signature:

- disciplined distance;
- compressed stillness before commitment;
- decisive entry on one line;
- strong ending posture and breath.

Best for:

- tension;
- minimal clean sword beats;
- ritualized confrontation.

### Iaido-Inspired

Visual signature:

- draw and first cut are one continuous event;
- scabbard hand and hip turn matter visually;
- minimal wasted motion;
- controlled finish.

### Chinese Jian-Inspired

Visual signature:

- agile point control;
- precise wrist-led redirection supported by body steps;
- lighter, more continuous lines than a heavy saber.

Best for:

- elegant technical characters;
- narrow blades and flowing costume.

### Chinese Dao-Inspired

Visual signature:

- broader cutting arcs;
- waist and shoulder follow-through;
- blade momentum continues through turns;
- stronger chopping/sweeping silhouette than jian.

### Spear / Qiang-Inspired

Visual signature:

- rear hand powers, lead hand guides;
- tip owns the threat line;
- linear thrusts contrast with circular recovery;
- stance and weapon axis remain readable.

### Staff / Gun / Bo-Inspired

Visual signature:

- hands slide or exchange roles;
- the shaft creates leverage;
- figure-eight, thrust, block, and sweep share one continuous spatial system.

### Naginata / Glaive-Inspired

Visual signature:

- long crescent cutting path;
- lower stance;
- earlier startup and longer brake than a spear thrust;
- body turns before the distant blade completes the arc.

### Kali / Arnis / Eskrima-Inspired

Visual signature:

- compact diagonal lines;
- alternating hand rhythm;
- weapon and empty hand have separate jobs;
- rapid range transitions.

Best for:

- sticks, short blades, paired props, tight choreography.

### Silat-Inspired

Visual signature:

- low entries;
- off-line angles;
- close weapon/body integration;
- sudden level changes and compact finishing silhouettes.

Best for:

- stealth, asymmetry, and confined environments.

### Flexible Weapons

Use rope-dart, meteor-hammer, whip, or chain mechanics as the basis:

- handle initiates;
- weighted end arrives later;
- orbit and tension must remain readable;
- recovery manages the returning wave.

## 5. Movement And Hybrid Systems

### Parkour

Visual signature:

- obstacle contact is explicit;
- momentum is preserved through vault, wall, landing, roll, and continued run;
- route is the opponent.

### Wushu Performance Vocabulary

Visual signature:

- extended lines;
- clear peaks;
- large costume-readable arcs;
- controlled aerial accents;
- strong final silhouettes.

Use as cinematic amplification, not as the only physical base for contact-heavy fights.

### Dance Integration

Use when relationship and rhythm matter more than damage:

- shared center;
- counterbalance;
- lead-follow exchange;
- mirrored or delayed phrases;
- contact transitions.

### Creature / Robot Hybrid

Map nonhuman anatomy onto a real function:

```text
boxing pressure + hydraulic shoulder
wrestling drive + quadruped traction
staff leverage + mechanical tail
bagua circling + multi-leg body orientation
```

Do not copy a human style name without adapting support points, joint limits, mass, and reach.

## 6. Mixing Rules

Use one primary system and at most one secondary system per short beat.

Good combinations:

- boxing footwork + Wing Chun close interception;
- wrestling pressure + judo turning throw;
- spear body line + Xingyi-inspired forward intent;
- Baguazhang-inspired circling + flexible weapon orbit;
- Muay Thai pressure + Silat-inspired low exit;
- HEMA body/blade contact + fantasy mechanical weapon function;
- parkour route mechanics + mobile archery.

Bad combinations:

- five martial-art names competing for the same movement role;
- switching posture and rhythm every half-second;
- mixing rooted heavy style with weightless acrobatics without a transition;
- using camera effects to fake a style distinction.

For two-character contrast:

```text
agile role: circular/off-axis/continuous grammar
heavy role: linear/rooted/burst-and-settle grammar
```

Their actions should interlock causally while keeping different silhouettes and recovery signatures.

## 7. Prompt Template

```text
Martial basis: [primary] with [optional secondary] only.
Visible signature: [stance, range, force path, rhythm].
Support and center: [feet/base/level/weight shift].
Action line: [body path + weapon path].
Opponent response: [evade/block/absorb/lose/convert].
Recovery signature: [snap back/flow through/root/angle out].
Fantasy amplification: [one prop function or one cinematic exaggeration].
Avoid: [style-specific failure].
```

Example:

```text
Use a Bajiquan-inspired mechanical signature, not a generic punch combo: low stable base, one compact forward entry, shoulder and hip arriving as a single armored frame, the opponent displaced backward while the synthetic fighter's rear foot and floor absorb the recoil. End in a rooted square stance. No floating lunge, no long windup, no repeated chain punches, and no camera shake replacing body collision.
```

## 8. Failure Controls

| Failure | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| every style looks like kickboxing | restate stance, distance, force path, rhythm, and recovery |
| named style becomes cosplay pose | retain the name and add its stance, path, rhythm, support, and recovery signature |
| acrobatics float | add takeoff, support, apex, landing, and immediate recovery |
| throws teleport | add off-balance, contact, leverage, fall arc, and end positions |
| soft style becomes weak | add opponent pressure and visible redirection consequence |
| heavy style becomes slow idle | compress preload, keep one explosive peak, preserve long recovery |
| weapon style ignores weapon mass | apply `weapon-body-dynamics.md` before the martial signature |
| robot copies human anatomy badly | remap force chain to actual joints, actuators, and support points |
