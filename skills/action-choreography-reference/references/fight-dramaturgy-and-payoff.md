# Fight Dramaturgy And Payoff

Use this reference for a multi-beat fight whose interest depends on reversals, a threatened objective, a planted tactical advantage, an environmental affordance, a weapon condition, or a finishing opportunity. It owns the **combat-balance arc and visible promise/payoff ledger**. It does not replace physical choreography, shot continuity, timing, sound, or platform syntax.

## 1. Define the combat balance

Here, balance is dramatic and tactical, not only biomechanical balance.

```text
objective: what is being protected, reached, escaped, captured, delayed, or denied?
initiative owner: who currently forces the other to respond?
active advantage: reach, mass, speed, terrain, information, formation, weapon state, or objective access
pressure cost: what is becoming harder, narrower, damaged, exposed, or exhausted?
reversal source: what observable condition could change initiative?
```

A fight beat either sustains pressure while changing cost, or changes the combat balance. If it changes only poses and effects, it is display rather than dramatic progression.

Do not force a reversal into every exchange. Too many unsupported turns erase hierarchy and make the fight feel arbitrary. Use a reversal only when the previous beat created its source.

## 2. Create a visible promise

A promise is a viewer-accessible cue whose later use or failure matters:

- an exposed route, weak support, damaged guard, unstable ledge, hanging chain, steam valve, dropped weapon, limited ammunition, failing light, hostage/objective, approaching opponent, or repeated recovery habit;
- a visible gaze, sound, touch, obstruction, failed attempt, or spatial relation that makes the cue legible before payoff;
- enough screen time and contrast for the audience to register it, but not a pause that kills active pressure.

Record backstage:

| Promise | Visible seed | Expected tactical use | Payoff window | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | what the viewer actually sees/hears | what possibility it creates | which later beat can use it | seeded / delayed / paid / reversed / retired |

Only retain promises that change a later choice. Decorative props and unexplained close-ups do not enter the ledger.

## 3. Pay it off causally

Four valid payoff families:

- **confirmation**: the seeded advantage works and changes initiative, route, weapon state, or objective access;
- **reversal**: the opponent anticipates or corrupts the expected use, producing a justified setback;
- **partial payoff**: the promise solves one problem but creates a new cost;
- **transfer**: the same object, route, weakness, or rhythm changes owner and becomes the opponent's tool.

The payoff must inherit the original cue's object, direction, state, or sound. Do not introduce a decisive prop, power, ally, or route at the moment of use if the viewer had no earlier access to it.

If a seeded promise is no longer useful, retire it visibly or remove the setup. Do not leave an emphasized advantage unresolved by accident.

## 4. Protect active suspense

During an unresolved threat, insert a reaction, close-up, flash, or cutaway only when it does at least one job:

- reveals new threat information;
- changes initiative or available response;
- measures cost, distance, timing, or consequence;
- seeds or pays a promise;
- bridges continuity while preserving the active vector.

An unrelated reaction that merely repeats fear or admiration interrupts the pressure chain. Hand shot necessity to `$shot-information-progression` and cut mechanics to `$cinematic-audiovisual-language`.

## 5. Build the peak from consequence, not ritual formula

The decisive beat should carry the sequence's strongest **evidence allocation**, not a mandatory number of windups, close-ups, flashes, or seconds.

```text
earlier promise becomes actionable
-> commitment closes alternatives
-> contact / near miss / objective action is readable
-> receiver, weapon, route, or environment changes
-> the combat balance cannot return without a new visible source
```

Scale preparation, hit-stop, reaction length, camera accent, sound, and environmental response to mass, genre, framing, and narrative consequence. A quiet disarm, dropped object, failed grip, blocked exit, or silence can be more decisive than a larger explosion.

## 6. Compact handoff

Pass downstream:

```text
opening combat balance:
active pressure and objective:
promise seed(s):
escalation cost:
reversal source:
payoff type and visible consequence:
final initiative / route / weapon / objective state:
```

The final generation prompt should express this as visible action causality. Do not paste ledger labels, theory terms, or promises that cannot be seen or heard.

## Boundaries

- For one isolated strike with no setup/payoff burden, use the normal action-physics chain instead.
- For a pure weapon or ability showcase without an opponent/objective contest, use `$action-showcase-direction`.
- For macro story structure beyond the fight, use `$video-structure-design` and `$screenwriting-story-craft`.
- Do not equate longer windup with greater force, longer reaction with greater impact, or tighter facial framing with greater power.
- Do not make a tactical cue obvious only through analysis text; the audience must receive it in picture or sound.
