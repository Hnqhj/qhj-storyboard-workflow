# Close-Exchange Density

Read this reference when a close-range fight, hand exchange, weapon bind, clinch, or short-control sequence feels like turn-taking, slow demonstration, unreadable flailing, or uniform-speed hitting. It owns the **density envelope and punctuation pattern**. Body mechanics and defensive answers come from `$action-choreography-reference`; shot design comes from `$cinematic-audiovisual-language`.

## 1. Density Is Relationship Change, Not Hits Per Second

Count readable changes in the interaction:

```text
threat or probe
-> contact / near miss / interception
-> defensive answer
-> pressure, angle, balance, grip, line, or distance changes
-> next action uses that change
```

A flurry with six limb motions but no changed relationship is low-density noise. Two clear exchanges that steal a line, break a guard, and change initiative can feel faster and more dangerous.

Use medium-close or close coverage only when it can prove the relevant hands, torso, weapon line, expression, or contact point. Ask the camera specialist for wider recovery or consequence proof when the new body state would otherwise be hidden.

## 2. Three Rhythm Families

Choose one family for a short phrase. The counts are flexible.

### Cluster -> Accent

```text
two to four short probes/interceptions
-> one stronger committed contact
-> visible receiver correction or displacement
```

Use when fast technical exchanges should build toward one readable punctuation. The short contacts establish cadence; the accent changes state.

### Acceleration -> Break

```text
cadence compresses
-> opponent interrupts the expected rhythm
-> level, angle, support, grip, or route changes sharply
-> action continues from the break
```

Use when uniform speed makes the exchange flat. The break may be a jam, catch, slip, body check, balance loss, weapon bind, or abrupt disengagement; it should not be an unrelated pose or effect.

### Hold -> Breach -> Payoff

```text
defense visibly holds under pressure
-> a specific line/support/control point fails
-> the matching action enters that opening
-> consequence proves why the breach mattered
```

Use when the audience must understand a reversal or finishing opportunity. The opening must be visible before the payoff; do not label it only in analysis text.

## 3. Convert The Pattern Into Natural Action

Internal rhythm labels do not belong in the final prompt. Convert them to a causal phrase:

```text
A's first short attack draws B's guard inward; the next contact pins that arm just long enough for A to change angle. B braces and recovers the line, but the recovery step narrows the stance, letting A's heavier follow-up drive both bodies into a new spacing.
```

For each short contact, name only what the shot can prove:

```text
initiator and visible line:
receiver answer:
contact/near-miss proof:
small state change:
source of the next action:
```

Do not force a long named-move list, mandatory elbow, arbitrary body-part rotation, or an effect on every contact. Style identity should determine which limbs, weapon functions, levels, and responses are natural.

## 4. Timing Confidence

Use relative timing unless a real audio, edit, animation, mocap, or previs clock exists:

```text
readable entry -> compressed exchange cluster -> brief punctuation
-> receiver/consequence registration -> recovery or next phrase
```

Keep the burst short enough that contact relations remain readable. If the generator collapses the phrase, first reduce required contacts or split the clip; do not add invented microsecond values.

### Bounded Post-Retime Test

When an inspected output has correct contact order, body mechanics, continuity, and consequences but the entire close exchange is uniformly too slow, a bounded editorial speed change may be tested as one variable before rewriting the choreography.

```text
eligible: causality correct, motion continuous, no missing action, only cadence too slow
test: one modest retime range, with sound and motion blur reviewed together
compare: original versus retimed version at the same in/out points
reject: interpolation artifacts, unreadable contact, broken audio, or lost weight
```

Archive and evaluate the original first. Do not use retiming to hide a wrong range, missing defense, teleport, pose reset, or broken anatomy. Record successful retime evidence in `$creative-casebook`; it remains case-specific until repeated results justify a shared rule.

## 5. Camera Handoff

Give `$cinematic-audiovisual-language` a proof request, not a fixed shot recipe:

```text
exchange proof: hands/weapon line and both answers remain readable
punctuation proof: decisive contact or break is visible
consequence proof: changed balance, spacing, grip, initiative, or environment registers
continuity: screen direction, body phase, and contact owner survive any cut
```

One continuous medium-close shot may be strongest. Use an insert only if the break/contact point cannot otherwise be read. A close-to-medium-to-wide chain is optional, not mandatory.

## Quality Gate

- Each short contact has a visible answer or tactical effect.
- The phrase changes at least one line, angle, balance, grip, distance, initiative, or route.
- One punctuation event is stronger than the surrounding contacts.
- The receiver and consequence receive screen time.
- Exact timing appears only with an actual clock or inspected evidence.
- Post-retime is offered only when cadence is the sole diagnosed fault.
