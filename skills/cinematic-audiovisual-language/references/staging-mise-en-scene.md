# Staging And Mise-En-Scene

Use this when blocking, frame composition, city/world scale, color relation, costume, props, and power dynamics matter.

## Mise-En-Scene In Practice

Treat mise-en-scene as everything arranged for the camera: set, location, architecture, lighting, costume, props, actor position, body direction, depth, and movement inside the frame.

Do not describe these as decoration. Tie them to story function:

```text
the environment makes the subject feel tiny
the costume makes the subject cut against the world
the lighting reveals danger from behind
the prop changes who controls the scene
```

## Blocking Before Camera

First decide what the subject does in space. Then choose where the camera should be.

Good blocking answers:

- Who moves first?
- Who/what blocks or reveals the subject?
- What is closer to camera than the subject?
- What crosses the frame to hide a cut?
- What changes between the start and end of the shot?

For weighted action also answer:

- Where is the body center relative to the support area?
- Which foot, hand, wall, vehicle, cable, or ground anchor receives the load?
- Is the prop grip-biased, balanced, tip-heavy, head-heavy, or flexible-delayed?
- Where is there enough room for follow-through and braking?
- Which object or surface receives the equal-and-opposite response?

## Depth Planes

Build frames with three readable layers:

- Foreground: obstruction, speed streak, frame-within-frame, danger near lens.
- Midground: main subject and readable action.
- Background: scale, route, threat, social world, weather, light source.

In AI video prompts, state what stays sharp and what blurs:

```text
foreground architecture streaks past with directional blur, subject remains sharp, background city compresses into warm haze
```

## Composition As Meaning

- Scale contrast: subject small against a huge system.
- Negative space: loneliness, threat, waiting, fate.
- Diagonal vector: speed, aggression, escape, collision.
- Symmetry: control, ritual, institutional power.
- Broken symmetry: rebellion, intrusion, disturbance.
- Foreground occlusion: surveillance, danger, depth, concealment.
- Frame-within-frame: trap, display, performance, isolation.

## Color Relation

Use color as a relationship, not a palette:

- subject color vs world color
- warm safety vs cold intrusion
- sterile city vs dirty human texture
- red warning vs blue control
- pale environment vs black silhouette

For "opposite color" requests, specify who owns each color:

```text
world owns warm ivory, coral, gold; character owns black, chrome, electric cyan
```

## Camera Movement Must Reveal Or Transform

Use camera movement only if it changes what the viewer knows or feels:

- push in: commitment, recognition, pressure
- pull out: isolation, scale, helplessness
- tracking: pursuit, momentum, continuity of motion
- tilt up: power, reveal height, launch
- overhead: map, trap, choreography
- orbit: power shift or unstable relation
- whip pan: sudden directional transfer

If a camera move does not reveal, track, hide, or transform something, use a simpler shot.
