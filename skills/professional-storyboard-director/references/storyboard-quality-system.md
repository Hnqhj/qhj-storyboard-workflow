# Storyboard Quality System

## Professional Frame Job

Each frame must do at least one clear job:

- orient location, scale, screen direction, or subject count;
- reveal intent, attention, decision, vulnerability, status, threat, or desire;
- show process, contact, transformation, usage, or material truth;
- conceal information deliberately;
- transfer initiative, force, emotion, or viewer attention;
- resolve, invert, or loop the opening image.

If a shot has only a cool angle and no job, replace it or merge it.

## Information Ladder

Use an information ladder before shot count:

```text
hook or orientation
-> subject intent
-> pressure or obstacle
-> decision or turn
-> consequence
-> memory frame, release, or loop
```

For 5 seconds: use 2-4 frames.
For 10-15 seconds: use 5-8 frames.
For 30 seconds: use 8-14 frames.
For 60 seconds: use chapters, not a single long shot list.

## Shot Ledger

For multi-shot work, check adjacent shots with this ledger:

```text
Beat / function / shot size / camera height / angle / lens feel / camera movement / screen direction / subject action / new information / cut reason / sound cue
```

Revise when two adjacent shots repeat four or more columns without deliberate rhythm.

## Spatial Continuity

Lock these before designing style:

- subject start and end position;
- screen direction and action axis;
- foreground, midground, background hierarchy;
- where the viewer's eye should land before and after each cut;
- which shot resets geography after close-ups, occlusion, or subjective distortion.

Use inserts and close-ups to intensify detail, not to hide missing geography forever.

## Planning Evidence Trio

Choose planning artifacts by the question that must be proven. They are complementary, not interchangeable:

| Evidence | Proves | Required when |
|---|---|---|
| **Storyboard** | viewer-facing frame intent, information order, composition, and rhythm | any sequence where frame choice or cut order matters |
| **Overhead / camera diagram** | physical actor/object positions, camera zones, axes, paths, obstacles, and relevant light areas | geography, moving axes, multi-person blocking, action, or camera paths are easy to misread |
| **Shot list** | capture/generation coverage, settings, ownership, order, version, and handoff | multiple units, production accounting, or downstream generation/editing handoff is required |

Load only the evidence demanded by risk:

```text
simple single setup -> storyboard/frame plan only
spatially complex sequence -> add overhead/camera diagram
multi-unit production or generation handoff -> add shot list
```

Do not let one artifact impersonate another: a beautiful board does not prove physical feasibility, an overhead does not prove viewer rhythm, and a shot list does not prove either composition or geography.

## Rhythm Curve

A professional sequence has a density curve:

- orientation usually breathes longer;
- escalation compresses shot duration or increases contrast;
- peak gets the most precise frame job;
- aftermath either widens, holds, or cuts to a changed detail.

Do not distribute intensity evenly unless the concept is monotony, ritual, surveillance, or mechanical repetition.

## Frame Design Checklist

For each important frame, decide:

- dominant silhouette;
- primary line of action or gaze;
- foreground obstruction or clean access;
- negative space ownership;
- depth layers;
- camera height and psychological relation;
- lens feel: wide, normal, compressed, macro/detail, subjective;
- lighting or color state if it affects story;
- motion or transition out of the frame.

## Production Notes

Use concise, production-like language. A storyboard is not a prose scene:

- visible action over mood labels;
- frame mechanics over abstract praise;
- cut reason over decorative transitions;
- sound cue only when it changes attention, timing, or meaning.

For AI video, keep the shot count feasible and state which details must remain locked across cuts.
