# Imported Suspense Storyboard Control

Use this reference for 悬疑、惊悚、窥视、心理悬念、结构悬念、暴力爆发前蓄力, or any request where composition, lighting, camera movement, editing, sound, VFX, and transitions must be designed as one suspense machine rather than separate decorations.

This distills the supplied local PDF `顶级悬疑导演分镜运镜·构图光影一体化训练手册（3天精通版）(1).pdf`.

## 1. Core operating idea

Treat every shot as a suspense-control machine:

```text
suspense goal
-> information state
-> composition/light/camera/edit/sound/VFX/transition controls
-> one unified viewer experience
```

Never add a camera move, shadow, sound, or transition only because it looks professional. It must control what the audience knows, expects, fears, or misunderstands.

## 2. Pick the suspense type before the shot language

| Suspense type | Viewer feeling | Primary control variables |
|---|---|---|
| Psychological | anxiety, unease, premonition | information gap, POV, timing |
| Voyeuristic | spying, being watched, intrusion | subjective view, frame limits, shadow, obstruction |
| Surreal | uncanny, disordered, irrational | spatial logic, color, sound mismatch |
| Structural | puzzle, confusion, time fracture | timeline, crosscutting, information architecture |
| Violent | standoff, pressure before impact | dialogue tempo, object insert, delayed release |

Use only the type(s) that serve the scene. A quiet clue scene should not become a generic horror montage.

## 3. Three-layer breakdown

### Task layer

Answer before writing shots:

- What does the audience know / not know?
- What does the character know / not know?
- What emotion should be produced now?
- When is information released, and how much?

### Structure layer

Map the task into controllable modules:

- composition: what is shown, hidden, framed, or withheld;
- lighting: what is revealed, concealed, cut by shadow, or falsely warmed;
- camera: how attention and psychological distance change;
- editing: when information appears, repeats, delays, or collides;
- sound: what is heard before/after/without image;
- VFX: how perception is distorted or sharpened;
- transition: how two shots psychologically connect.

### Language layer

Replace empty prestige words with executable details:

- not "cinematic suspense";
- use "door-frame occlusion covers the left third, the subject is a small silhouette in the far-right warm spill, the camera slow-pushes 20 cm as the offscreen knock repeats."

## 4. Composition controls

Use composition to select information:

- **frame restriction**: door crack, window grid, keyhole, monitor, reflection; creates peeking and partial knowledge.
- **imbalance**: subject trapped on one side with heavy empty darkness; suggests order about to break.
- **low angle**: pressure, dominance, threat scale.
- **high angle**: isolation, weakness, trapped geography.
- **symmetry**: cold control, institutional pressure, ritual.
- **foreground obstruction**: implies hidden observer or compromised access.
- **negative space**: invites the audience to imagine threat outside the visible area.

Shot size functions:

- wide/long: space, isolation, environmental threat;
- full/medium: body relation, action continuity, geography;
- close: emotional pressure and withheld context;
- extreme close: clue, body reaction, compressed threat, missing whole.

## 5. Lighting controls

Use light as information release:

- **side/Rembrandt-like light**: split inner state, moral ambiguity, secret.
- **silhouette/backlight**: unknown identity or unreadable threat.
- **top light**: ritual, institutional coldness, eye sockets, inhuman mood.
- **underlight**: distortion, fear, uncanny face or object.
- **slit light**: door/window gap, surveillance, cut-up space.
- **neon/mixed light**: disordered reality, dream logic, urban paranoia.

Parameter levers:

- contrast ratio: higher contrast -> stronger unknown/pressure;
- shadow percentage: more darkness -> more unseen threat;
- color temperature: cold = alienation; warm can be false safety;
- quality: hard light = sharp danger; soft light = ambiguity;
- motivation: source must be plausible when realism matters: lamp, TV, moon, streetlight, flashlight, furnace, phone.

Common fixes:

- too bright -> reduce exposure, increase shadow area, isolate the known zone;
- flat light -> move to side/back/slot source and raise contrast;
- unmotivated light -> attach every source to a visible or implied practical;
- color chaos -> reduce to 2-3 active colors and assign each a narrative job;
- dead eyes -> preserve a tiny motivated catchlight even in low key scenes.

## 6. Lens and camera movement

Use lens and movement to set mental distance:

- 14-35mm: warped space, proximity, pursuit, claustrophobia, surreal pressure.
- 35-50mm: grounded human observation, dialogue, procedural clues.
- 85mm+: surveillance, compression, isolation, trapped faces.
- macro: clue, object, wound-free impact detail, breath, skin, mechanism.

Movement functions:

- very slow push: dread and expectation; best when viewers wait for meaning.
- handheld: instability, loss of control, bodily proximity.
- tracking/follow: alignment with character route or fear.
- pan/tilt: controlled reveal or searching eye.
- vertical rise/drop: power or vulnerability shift.
- dolly-zoom / perceptual distortion: psychological break; use only when the whole scene supports it.
- long take: time pressure, ritual, inescapable observation.

Speed code:

- almost imperceptible = anxiety;
- slow = oppressive or ritual;
- medium = neutral narrative;
- fast = urgency or confusion;
- sudden = shock or violent rupture.

## 7. Suspense rhythm curve

Design the sequence before individual shots:

```text
setup -> build-up -> delay -> threshold -> reveal/rupture -> aftermath
```

Editing tools:

- crosscutting: time pressure between lines of action;
- match cut: hidden relation, surreal link, thematic echo;
- jump cut: disordered time or mental fracture;
- J-cut: incoming sound creates expectation before image;
- L-cut: previous sound contaminates the next image;
- freeze/slow motion: isolate the key fact or stretch the moment before/after impact.

## 8. Sound and VFX as suspense controls

Sound jobs:

- offscreen sound defines unseen space;
- silence can be an active pressure zone before a reveal;
- repeated small sound can become a timer;
- J/L sound bridges can misdirect or extend emotion;
- a sound can reveal more than the frame, but must remain motivated.

VFX jobs:

- lens distortion, focus pull, flicker, reflection warp, slow motion, time smear, double exposure, or glitch only when perception itself is the suspense subject.
- Avoid VFX that hides the information the shot is supposed to reveal.

## 9. Director-system shortcuts

Use these as compact style-routing mechanisms, not name decoration:

- **Hitchcock-like psychological suspense**: audience information gap, subjective anticipation, delayed reveal.
- **Fincher-like voyeur/control suspense**: precise framing, restricted views, cold practical light, procedural objects.
- **Lynch-like surreal suspense**: broken spatial logic, sound unease, color dislocation, dream/reality seam.
- **Nolan-like structural suspense**: parallel timelines, puzzle release, visual rules for each time state.
- **Tarantino-like violent delay**: dialogue pressure, object insert, silence before rupture, impact aftermath.

Assign each name a job if used in output.

## 10. Suspense quality gate

Before finalizing, score internally:

- suspense creation: does the shot actually produce unknown, anxiety, or expectation?
- emotional precision: do all elements point to the same feeling?
- visual logic: why this composition, light, camera, and cut?
- shootability: can this be filmed or generated without impossible instructions?
- rhythm: does the pressure build, delay, rupture, and breathe?
- clue/detail: are there controlled details or foreshadowing, not random clutter?

If a suspense shot only sounds impressive but does not alter information state, rewrite it.
