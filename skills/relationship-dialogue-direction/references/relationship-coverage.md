# Relationship Coverage Contract

Use this reference for two-person romance, confrontation, apology, confession, breakup, reconciliation, and farewell scenes.

## 1. Contract Before Coverage

Write these facts before selecting shots:

```text
A wants:
B wants:
What each person refuses to say:
Start power/distance:
End power/distance:
Shared anchor: door, table, letter, weapon, railing, bed edge, threshold, or empty space.
```

An emotional turn must be visible. Use a step closer, withdrawal, broken eyeline, released grip, untouched object, interrupted breath, lowered weapon, or changed speaking rhythm.

## 2. Performance Relay

Give the speaker a tactic and the listener a delayed answer.

| Beat | Initiator | Receiver | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Containment | avoids, softens, challenges, or withholds | watches, holds breath, or protects a prop | relationship baseline |
| Pressure | asks, corners, leaves, or admits | delays, shifts weight, loses eyeline, or tightens grip | imbalance |
| Turn | stops, touches, refuses, or reveals | receives, resists, then changes one behavior | new status |
| Residue | stays or exits | follows, does not follow, or returns to stillness | ending state |

Do not assign identical gestures, simultaneous head turns, or continuous crying to both performers.

## 3. Coverage Ladder

Use only the shots that prove the relationship change.

1. **Master two-shot**: Establish screen sides, distance, shared anchor, and who controls the space.
2. **Pressure OTS**: Keep the near shoulder as a relationship boundary; show the speaking tactic and the other person's gaze target.
3. **Reaction close-up**: Cut only when the receiver's micro-action changes the scene.
4. **Exchange reverse**: Change angle or distance enough to show the power shift while preserving axis and eyeline.
5. **Consequence two-shot or held detail**: Show the changed distance, a released/retained prop, or an unresolved exit line.

Use a detail insert only when it carries the turn: fingers releasing a sleeve, a cup left untouched, a letter folded away, a hand stopping at a door, or a pendant held but not given.

## 4. Axis And Framing Locks

- Establish left/right positions before close coverage.
- Hold eyeline height across reverse shots unless a deliberate status change motivates the difference.
- Preserve the near shoulder side in OTS shots; change it only with a master reset, action match, foreground wipe, or explicit camera cross.
- Let a wider frame prove body distance; let close framing prove a single eye, breath, jaw, hand, or swallow change.
- Reserve camera movement for a relationship event: a slow push as defense fails, a pull-back as distance becomes final, or a lock-off hold after a truth lands.

## 5. Prompt Packaging

For a keyframe, write the static relationship state: who faces whom, who looks away, distance, prop ownership, hand state, and light direction. Do not include dialogue, timed action, or camera movement.

For video, write one continuous change:

```text
Start: [A/B relation and body state].
Action: [one person's tactic] while [the other's delayed reception].
Camera: [one motivated move or lock-off].
End: [changed distance, gaze, prop, or status].
Keep: [axis, screen side, eyeline, identity, wardrobe, shared anchor].
```

## 6. QA

- A viewer can tell the relationship before the first close-up.
- The listener has at least one specific nonverbal response.
- Each cut carries a new emotional fact.
- The final frame cannot be confused with the opening relationship state.
