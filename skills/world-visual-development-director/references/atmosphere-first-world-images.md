# Atmosphere-First World Images

Use this reference for high-aesthetic world images, poetic megastructures, myth explained through technology, uninhabited environments, and any result that is coherent but emotionally flat.

## Core Judgment

The image must work at three reading distances:

1. **Thumbnail:** emotion, value pattern, negative space, dominant silhouette.
2. **Normal view:** scale, light event, environmental depth, world relationship.
3. **Close view:** material evidence, cultural causality, technological explanation.

Never reverse this order. Close-view detail cannot rescue a weak thumbnail.

## Frame Recipe

Lock only the choices that materially shape the mood:

```text
primary emotion
dominant empty field
single governing silhouette
single environmental or light event
three broad value masses
restrained color relationship
depth medium and edge-loss pattern
one second-read world mechanism
localized detail zone
```

Useful emotional targets include solemn, numinous, desolate, serene, expectant, haunted, sacred, oppressive, or quietly sublime. Translate the chosen emotion into visible conditions rather than leaving it as an adjective.

## Composition Rules

- Use asymmetry, off-center balance, deep foreground framing, distant scale, or horizon suppression when they strengthen mood.
- Reserve large calm regions. Negative space may carry sky, shadow, mist, water, sand, cloud, darkness, or architectural void.
- Keep one clear dominant subject or spatial event. Repeated monuments, equal-strength objects, and uniformly busy skylines dilute awe.
- Use scale proof without forcing a person into the frame: terrain strata, weather layers, waterlines, windows, maintenance lights, birds, tree belts, bridges, or shadow span.
- For boards, vary emotional distance and spatial function, not just camera angle. Give each frame its own silhouette and light logic while maintaining a shared world grammar.

## Light And Color

- Use one motivated light event: a horizon burn, shaft through haze, reflected water glow, eclipsed source, storm charge, ritual energy field, moonlit cloud break, or infrastructure emission.
- Let broad dark, middle, and light masses carry the image before surface detail.
- Prefer two dominant color families plus one restrained accent. Allow temperature contrast to carry emotion.
- Preserve black and low-information shadow where appropriate; do not lift every surface for readability.
- Use bloom, halation, diffusion, grain, or analog softness only when they support the atmosphere and do not erase structural clarity.

## World And Technology Integration

- Make technology alter geography, weather, tides, celestial motion, ritual behavior, transport, agriculture, energy flow, or the scale of habitation.
- Hide part of the explanation. Let the machine first resemble a mountain, gate, sun, dragon, tree, ark, altar, or celestial phenomenon.
- Reveal engineering through seams, maintenance paths, energy gradients, structural rhythm, controlled emissions, or material transitions on second inspection.
- Prefer one consequential mechanism over many decorative devices.

## Detail Budget

Allocate detail by narrative importance:

- high detail: focal interface or governing mechanism;
- medium detail: nearby surfaces that establish material and use;
- low detail: atmosphere, distant massing, shadow fields, and nonessential architecture.

If every surface is equally sharp, patterned, illuminated, or mechanically exposed, simplify before regenerating.

## Nine-Grid Rhythm

Treat a 3x3 board as one visual album:

- establish a shared atmosphere family and material grammar;
- distribute warm, cool, and night frames deliberately rather than randomly;
- alternate open and dense compositions, near and far scale, stillness and environmental force;
- make every panel reveal a different world function or mythic mechanism;
- reject panels that repeat the same monument silhouette or generic concept-art staging.

## Failure Diagnosis

| Failure | Correction |
|---|---|
| Technically impressive but emotionally flat | Remove secondary structures; enlarge the empty field; strengthen one light event. |
| Looks like a machine catalog | Hide mechanisms inside landscape, ritual, weather, or silhouette; keep only one explanatory detail zone. |
| Generic cinematic concept art | Choose a more specific emotional state, asymmetrical value pattern, and culturally caused world mechanism. |
| Too much fog, little depth | Restore overlap, scale anchors, ground relationships, contrast falloff, and material separation. |
| Nine panels feel unrelated | Reapply shared palette logic, atmosphere medium, material family, and recurring world law; keep panel-specific silhouettes. |
| Nine panels feel repetitive | Change spatial function, dominant geometry, environmental force, emotional distance, and light event—not only camera angle. |

## Final Gate

Before generation or delivery, ask:

1. What emotion arrives before explanation?
2. Where does the eye rest?
3. Which single silhouette governs the frame?
4. What is the motivated light event?
5. What carries depth besides fog?
6. What technological truth appears only on second reading?
7. Which details can be removed without weakening the world?

If these answers are vague, redesign the macro image before adding prompt adjectives.
