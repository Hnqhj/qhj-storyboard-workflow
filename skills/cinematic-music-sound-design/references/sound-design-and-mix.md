# Sound Design And Mix

## Sound Layers

1. Dialogue, narration, breath, vocal effort.
2. Ambience, room tone, weather, crowd bed, machine bed.
3. Foley: footsteps, clothing, body contact, handled objects.
4. Hard effects: doors, engines, impacts, weapons, machinery.
5. Designed effects: transformations, energy, subjective sound, impossible scale.
6. Music.
7. Silence and near-silence.

## Sound Object Description

For each important sound, define:

- source;
- material;
- action;
- size/weight;
- distance;
- direction;
- movement;
- environment;
- reflections/reverb;
- frequency emphasis;
- transient and decay;
- realism versus stylization.

Example:

```text
The motorcycle passes left-to-right close to camera: sharp chain transient,
compressed electric motor whine, low body resonance, short concrete reflections,
then a fast high-frequency Doppler tail.
```

## Perspective

- objective exterior;
- close subjective body perspective;
- helmet/interior muffling;
- tinnitus or impact filtering;
- distant observer;
- memory/dream perspective;
- source sound becoming score.

Perspective changes should follow story point of view.

## Sound-Image Relations

- sync: sound lands with image;
- anticipation: sound arrives before source appears;
- trail: sound continues after image cuts;
- bridge: one sound links locations or times;
- contradiction: calm music against violence;
- masking: loud event hides information;
- reveal: ambience drops to expose a small sound;
- substitution: expected impact replaced by silence or an intimate detail;
- motif: repeated sound gains narrative meaning.

## Mix Hierarchy

At each beat, select one primary listener focus:

- dialogue;
- source action;
- musical motif;
- environmental threat;
- subjective effect.

Support it with secondary layers and reduce competing frequency/dynamic content.

## Action Design

Impact is:

```text
preparation -> transient -> body/resonance -> debris/environment -> reaction -> tail
```

Avoid one generic boom for every hit. Vary material, size, distance, and consequence.

## Mass And Weapon Envelope

Build a weighted action as a short causal envelope:

```text
support/preload:
boot pressure, floor creak, cloth strain, breath, motor spool, cable tension

acceleration:
air movement, joint/armor movement, chain or blade pass, rising mechanical pitch

contact:
material-specific transient at the visible contact point

body/resonance:
low-mid vibration scaled to object size and construction

receiver/environment:
foot slide, body impact, suspension compression, wall/floor response, debris, water

braking/settling:
scrape, grip shift, cable return, armor rattle, blade ring, damper movement, breath release
```

Mass-distribution cues:

- grip-biased objects have shorter pass and settling tails;
- head-heavy objects have a stronger delayed body and longer brake/settle tail;
- flexible weapons produce a second delayed tension or return sound;
- giant or superhuman impacts should reach terrain and air, not become a louder generic boom;
- transformed weapons need a new acoustic handling signature after mass migration and lock.

Do not add all layers at equal volume. Choose one primary listener focus and let secondary consequences arrive with depth and delay.

## Silence

Silence can:

- create anticipation;
- reveal subjectivity;
- isolate dialogue;
- enlarge impact;
- mark shock;
- end a sequence without false resolution.

Use room tone or residual detail when absolute digital silence would feel artificial.
