# Music Control Vocabulary

Use musical controls that audibly change the result.

## Time

- tempo / BPM;
- free time or strict pulse;
- meter: 4/4, 3/4, 6/8, 5/4, 7/8;
- subdivision;
- swing or straight feel;
- syncopation;
- polyrhythm;
- metric displacement;
- accelerando or ritardando;
- perceived acceleration through density rather than literal tempo change.

## Rhythm

- ostinato;
- pulse;
- groove;
- clave-like pattern;
- motor rhythm;
- sparse attacks;
- triplet drive;
- dotted rhythm;
- stop-start rhythm;
- anticipation;
- impact rest;
- rhythmic fragmentation.

## Harmony

- tonal center;
- major/minor/modal language;
- pedal point;
- drone;
- open fifths;
- quartal harmony;
- chromatic planing;
- cluster;
- suspended harmony;
- ostinato harmony;
- harmonic rhythm;
- consonance/dissonance curve;
- modulation;
- unresolved cadence.

## Melody And Motif

- contour: rising, falling, arch, wave, static;
- interval identity;
- narrow or wide range;
- stepwise or angular movement;
- repetition with mutation;
- call and response;
- sequence;
- fragmentation;
- inversion;
- augmentation/diminution;
- register migration.

## Orchestration And Timbre

- strings: sul ponticello, sul tasto, harmonics, tremolo, spiccato, col legno;
- brass: muted, stopped, flutter tongue, low swells, heroic open tone;
- woodwinds: breathy, agile, fragile, multiphonics;
- percussion: taiko-like ensemble, frame drum, gran casa, metallic found objects, prepared percussion;
- piano/keys: felt piano, prepared piano, detuned upright, electric piano;
- electronic: granular texture, wavetable motion, FM metallic partials, analog drift, spectral freeze, side-chained pulse;
- voices: solo breath, choir cluster, whispered ensemble, wordless vocalise;
- cultural instruments: use with contextual research and avoid tokenistic decoration.

## Texture

- monophonic;
- homophonic;
- contrapuntal;
- layered ostinati;
- drone plus fragments;
- pointillistic;
- spectral cloud;
- acoustic/electronic hybrid;
- dense wall;
- exposed solo;
- negative space.

## Dynamics And Register

- dynamic envelope;
- crescendo by density;
- crescendo by register;
- transient peak;
- sub-bass restraint;
- high-register tension;
- low-register gravity;
- sudden subtraction;
- long decay;
- dry close sound versus distant reverberant sound.

## Production

- close-miked intimacy;
- room or hall perspective;
- mono source expanding to stereo;
- narrow stereo field;
- wide but stable image;
- saturation;
- tape flutter;
- bit reduction;
- sidechain breathing;
- filtered intro;
- frequency-band reveal;
- controlled distortion;
- transient shaping;
- reverb pre-delay and decay;
- clean headroom.

## Emotion Translation

Ask what kind of emotion:

- fear: stalking, panic, awe, dread, paranoia;
- sadness: numbness, regret, tenderness, grief, collapse;
- power: authority, rebellion, inevitability, triumph, menace;
- romance: intimacy, longing, innocence, danger, memory;
- hope: fragile, earned, naive, communal, transcendent.

Then choose controls. Do not rely on the label alone.
