# Film Scoring Workflow

## Spotting

Before composing, mark:

- cue in;
- cue out;
- dialogue-dense areas;
- no-music zones;
- hit points;
- transitions;
- subjective point-of-view changes;
- final cadence or sonic button.

Do not score every emotion already visible in the acting. Decide whether the score should:

- reinforce;
- foreshadow;
- contradict;
- reveal subtext;
- unify montage;
- transform a character/theme;
- create temporal momentum;
- provide release.

## Cue Architecture

For each cue, define:

```text
start state -> musical question -> development -> turn -> consequence -> exit
```

The cue exit matters. Options:

- resolve before dialogue;
- cut to silence;
- let a sustained tone bridge scenes;
- leave an unresolved harmony;
- end on a source sound;
- hand rhythm to the next scene.

## Motif And Leitmotif

A usable motif may be:

- interval pair;
- rhythmic cell;
- melodic contour;
- harmonic progression;
- timbre;
- pulse;
- recurring sound object.

Transform it through:

- tempo;
- mode;
- register;
- orchestration;
- inversion;
- augmentation/diminution;
- fragmentation;
- harmonic context;
- acoustic space.

Do not repeat a theme unchanged merely to label a character.

## Hit Points

Use synchronization selectively:

- first reveal;
- decisive contact;
- reversal;
- visual transformation;
- cut to title;
- emotional recognition;
- comic punctuation.

Too many hit points make the score feel mechanical. Let some visual events pass under a continuous musical phrase.

## Score Versus Source Music

- **Score**: non-diegetic dramatic music.
- **Source music**: music audible inside the story world.
- **Trans-diegetic transition**: source music expands into score or score collapses into a real source.

Use source/score transitions to change point of view or scale.
